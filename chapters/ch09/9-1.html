Lesson 9.1

Skill Spotlight: Critical & Creative Thinking

Pausing on purpose before you react, decide, or assume.

About 6 minutes 2 of 8 in this module
2
10 Essential Skills · #2

Critical & Creative Thinking

What this skill is

Not overthinking. Not arguing. Just pausing on purpose before you react, decide, or assume.

You make dozens of decisions every day, some small, some major. Much of the stress that comes with them doesn't come from the decision itself. It comes from confusion: not being sure what's actually going on, what matters most, or what's even true.

This module gives you a simple way to slow down, just for a moment, and think more clearly. That habit helps with more than schoolwork. It's the same thinking that builds an honest, specific STAR(T) story, and it's exactly the kind of thinking employers look for.

Benchmark thinking reacts. Milestone thinking pauses, checks, and decides.

Where you're headed

Here's the difference between where most students start and where you're headed, in plain terms.

See also

Lesson 1.2 explains Benchmark, Milestone, and Capstone.

  1. Benchmark Where you're starting

    • You describe a problem in general terms, based on whatever information you happen to have.
    • You acknowledge your own perspective, but don't always notice how it's shaping your thinking.
    • You come up with possible solutions, even if they're only loosely connected to the real problem.

  2. Milestone Your target

    • You figure out what's really going on by finding and using relevant information, not just what's easiest to find.
    • You seek out and consider other perspectives, not just your own first reaction.
    • You name the patterns, relationships, and context that matter, and use them to reach a clear, informed conclusion.

The shift from Benchmark to Milestone is the shift from reacting to checking: from going with your first read of a situation to actually examining it before you decide.

The three Milestone behaviors, up close

1Figure out what's really going on

At the Milestone level, you don't react to the first version of a problem you see. You pause long enough to ask, "What's actually happening here, and what do I need to know before I respond?"

This is the difference between assuming and checking. An assumption is something you treat as true without checking it. Milestone thinking notices the assumption and asks whether it's actually true, or just the first thing that came to mind.

Instead of "This assignment makes no sense," ask: "What is the professor actually asking me to do, and where can I check?"

2Look for other perspectives

Your first take on a situation is shaped by your experiences, your mood, and your assumptions, and it isn't always the full picture. Milestone thinking means considering how someone else might see the same situation, especially before you decide something is "obviously" true.

This isn't about doubting yourself constantly. It's about catching the moments when your read might be incomplete and asking what you might be missing.

Instead of assuming a coworker is ignoring you, ask: "Could they be busy or distracted? What else could explain this?"

3Name what matters, and why

This is where critical thinking becomes a tool you can use. It means being able to name the real question, what matters most in the situation, which information is solid and which is shaky, and what's likely to happen depending on what you choose. That's exactly what the 4C Check walks you through, and it's the same thinking that turns a vague STAR(T) story into one that's specific, honest, and worth telling.

"If I drop this class, what does that actually mean for my financial aid, my schedule next semester, and when I finish?" That's thinking through consequences, not guessing.

Where it shows up

Everyday decisions

Benchmark: You go with your gut and move on.

Milestone: You notice the thinking trap, ask what's actually true, and consider what matters most before deciding.

News and social media

Benchmark: If it sounds right, you accept it.

Milestone: You ask: Is this source credible? Is this fact, an ad, or someone's opinion?

Your STAR(T) story

Benchmark: You pick an experience because it sounds impressive and write something generic.

Milestone: You use the 4C Check to make sure your story is clear, accurate, and shows what you want it to show.

Using AI tools

Benchmark: Whatever AI gives you feels like an answer.

Milestone: You pause, check it against what you know, and decide whether it's useful before you use it.

In your own life

Reflect
  • Think of a time you reacted fast and later wished you'd paused. What would one good question have changed?
  • Who in your life sees situations very differently than you do? What could you learn from how they'd read a problem?
  • What's one decision in front of you right now that deserves a slower, more careful look?

How it shows up later

Critical thinking is a habit you build with practice, not a personality trait. You don't have to feel like "a critical thinker" for this to work. You just have to practice the pause: stop, ask one good question, then decide.

It's one of the most consistently valued skills in any job, any relationship, and any decision you'll face. And it's a strong choice for the fourth skill in your STAR(T) Stories.

Skill definition and Milestone indicators are based on the Kentucky Council on Postsecondary Education's Kentucky Graduate Profile (opens in a new tab).
