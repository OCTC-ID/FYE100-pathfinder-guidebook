Lesson 8.1

Skill Spotlight: Communication

Showing up in the conversation as someone worth listening to.

About 7 minutes 2 of 7 in this module
1
10 Essential Skills · #1

Communication

What this skill is

Not just talking. Not just writing. Showing up in the conversation as someone worth listening to.

Communication shows up everywhere, but most people don't think about it until something goes wrong. A missed appointment because you didn't follow up. An instructor who didn't know you were struggling until it was too late. An advising session that felt generic because you didn't come with real questions.

The conversations in this module, with your Success Coach, your instructors, and people in careers you're curious about, call for Milestone-level Communication: showing up as an active participant, not waiting to be told what to do.

Benchmark communication gets you by. Milestone communication gets you what you need.

Where you're headed

Here's the difference between where most students start and where you're headed, in plain terms.

See also

Lesson 1.2 explains Benchmark, Milestone, and Capstone.

  1. Benchmark Where you're starting

    • You ask questions to clarify your understanding.
    • You express thoughts and ideas in writing.
    • You share information in one-on-one and small-group conversations.

  2. Milestone Your target

    • You give focused attention, ask clarifying questions, and notice nonverbal signals.
    • You write formally and informally with logical structure, complete sentences, standard grammar, and correct punctuation.
    • You take part in formal conversations with peers and professionals by offering information and ideas.

The shift from Benchmark to Milestone is the shift from reacting to engaging: from answering when asked to showing up with something to contribute.

The three Milestone behaviors, up close

1Listen with focus, and ask clarifying questions

Listening at the Milestone level isn't passive. It means giving your full attention, not half-listening while you wait for your turn, noticing what's communicated beyond the words, and asking questions that show you processed what you heard.

A basic question checks a box: "When is the deadline?" A clarifying question shows real engagement: "I want to make sure I understand. Does the prerequisite need to be done before I apply to the program, or just before I start it?"

In an advising appointment, instead of nodding and hoping you understood, ask: "So just to confirm, if I take this course in the spring, I'm still on track to start the program in the fall?"

2Write with structure and care

Professional writing doesn't mean stiff. It means clear, organized, and respectful of the reader's time. At the Milestone level, you adjust to the situation: a text to a classmate looks different from an email to an instructor, which looks different from a message to an employer.

Where this breaks down most often for college students is email. A one-line message with no greeting, no context, and no clear ask puts the work on the reader. An email that introduces you, explains your situation, and asks a clear question gets a faster, more useful reply.

3Come to professional conversations with something to offer

This is the behavior most students underestimate. It doesn't mean being scripted. It means showing up as a participant, not just a recipient: you bring your research, your questions, your thinking, and your goals.

A student who says "I don't know what I want to take" is at Benchmark. A student who arrives with a draft schedule, two specific questions about course order, and a sense of their program direction is at Milestone, and gets a completely different kind of appointment.

The same goes for career conversations and any time you talk with someone in a professional role. Come with something. Leave with something.

Where it shows up in this module

This module puts you in the situations where the gap between Benchmark and Milestone is easiest to see.

Your advising appointment

Benchmark: You show up and wait to be told what to take.

Milestone: You bring your Academic Planner, your Career Coach results, and specific questions about course order, prerequisites, or program requirements. You offer your thinking and refine it with your coach.

A check-in with an instructor

Benchmark: You avoid the conversation and hope things improve.

Milestone: You send a clear, professional email that names the concern, says what you've tried, and asks a clear question. You make it easy for your instructor to help.

Career conversations

Benchmark: You ask "What do you do?" and listen.

Milestone: You ask questions based on your research: about the day-to-day reality, the career path, what surprised them about the field. You share your own thinking and leave with something concrete.

Group work

Benchmark: You wait to be given a role and do your part.

Milestone: You offer your strengths, notice when someone is struggling, ask clarifying questions about the task, and speak up when something isn't working, before it becomes a problem.

Pathfinder Tip

Before you send any professional email, read it once as the person receiving it. Does it give them enough context to help? Is the ask clear? Would you want to answer it? If not, revise before you send.

In your own life

Reflect
  • Think of a conversation that went better because you came prepared. What did you bring to it?
  • Is there an email or conversation you've been putting off? What would a clear, specific version of it sound like?
  • When someone explains something important to you, what helps you really listen, and what pulls your attention away?

How it shows up later

Communication is the skill underneath every other skill in this course. You need it to speak up for yourself in advising, to ask for help before it's too late, to present yourself to employers, and to tell your STAR(T) Stories in a way that's clear, specific, and worth listening to.

Every conversation, email, and appointment in this module is practice. You're not learning Communication in the abstract. You're doing it.

Skill definition and Milestone indicators are based on the Kentucky Council on Postsecondary Education's Kentucky Graduate Profile (opens in a new tab).
