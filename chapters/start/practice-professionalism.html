10 Essential Skills · #6

Professionalism

A story about following through, owning a mistake, or being someone others could count on.

Professionalism is about how you show up: following through, taking responsibility, managing your time and commitments, acting ethically, and communicating with care. Think of a moment when you showed reliability, integrity, or accountability in a real situation.

This is practice, and it isn't graded. Draft your story here, see how long it runs out loud, then record it or copy it into the matching STAR(T) Stories portal in Blackboard.

The STAR(T) Framework

Find Your Stories

Find your story

Your story can come from school, work, family, or your community. It doesn't have to be dramatic. It has to be real, and yours.

Mine your own work

Your Mile Markers may already hold a story worth telling.

Mile Marker #8: My Career Snapshot

Mile Marker #3: Managing My Time & Energy

Questions to help you find it:

  • Was there a time you met a deadline or commitment even when it was hard?
  • Have you ever owned a mistake, taken responsibility for something that went wrong, and handled it well?
  • Is there a moment when you juggled competing responsibilities and made deliberate choices about priorities?
  • Was there a time when doing the right thing was harder than taking the easy way out?

Draft it, one step at a time

Your draft saves in this browser as you go. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Using AI?

Ask it to help you find your story, not to write it: "Don't write my response for me. Help me find it." Your story has to sound like you.

STAR(T) Practice: Professionalism

Enter your name before saving.

Step 0Find your story first
SSituationSet the scene. What was going on?

Give just enough context for someone who wasn't there. Two or three sentences is usually plenty.

Watch out: The Situation is the setup, not the story. Get to the Task quickly.

TTaskDefine your role. What was yours to do?

What were you accountable for? What was expected of you, and what made it hard to meet?

Watch out: Your Task is what you were responsible for, not the group's general goal.

AActionShow what you did, step by step.

This is the most important step. Use "I": I decided, I reached out, I chose. Show the specific choices you made, especially when the easier choice would have been to do less, avoid the conversation, or let it slide. Describe what you actually did, not what you were supposed to do.

Watch out: "We figured it out" hides your part. Describe what you did, even inside a group effort.

RResultName the outcome. What happened because of what you did?

Results for this skill often show up in relationships: trust built, a problem solved, a standard kept. They don't have to be dramatic to matter.

Watch out: "It went well" isn't a result. Name what specifically happened.

TTransferConnect it forward. What does this mean for what comes next?

How will this shape the way you show up at work or in your program? Name a field, a role, or a situation where this skill will be tested.

Watch out: "This will help me in the future" isn't a Transfer. Name a specific place, role, or situation.
