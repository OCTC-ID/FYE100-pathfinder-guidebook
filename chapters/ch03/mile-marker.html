Mile Marker

Mile Marker #3: Managing My Time & Energy

The assignment, with support, and a form you can fill in and save.

About 12 minutes 9 of 10 in this module

You have the same 168 hours as everyone else, but you don't have the same brain, the same responsibilities, or the same energy reserves.

This Mile Marker isn't about building the perfect schedule. It's about starting to understand how you actually work: your patterns, your energy, and your limits. You'll come back to what you learn here when you build your capstone project.

●○AI use: AI-supported

You may use AI as a thinking partner to explore your ideas. Work with what it gives you, then write your responses in your own voice.

Respond to each question

Question 1

Your Patterns

Look at your Where My Time Goes log.

  • When were you most focused or productive? What was different about that time?
  • Where did time slip away? Name one pattern or time-waster you found.
  • Why do you think it happens, and what does it cost you?
A strong answer

Uses your log to name one real pattern or time-waster, explains why it happens, and says what it costs you. Not a general statement that could apply to anyone.

Question 2

Your Energy

Think about the five batteries: physical, mental, emotional, social, and spiritual.

  • Which one is most depleted right now?
  • What's draining it?
  • What is one small, realistic action you could take this week to start recharging it?
A strong answer

Names one battery, explains what's draining it in your life right now, and describes one small action that's realistic for this week.

Question 3

Your Week

Look at the week you mapped in My Week at a Glance.

  • What are your non-negotiables this week?
  • Where did you put your most important work, and why there?
  • What is one part of your plan that reflects what you value most?
A strong answer

Names your real non-negotiables, explains why your most important work landed where your energy is best, and connects one part of the plan to something you value.

Question 4

Your Self-Knowledge

This one doesn't have a wrong answer.

  • What is one thing you're learning about yourself this semester: about how your brain works, how your energy moves, or how your days tend to go? Why does it matter to pay attention to it?
  • After reading this module's lessons, did anything describe how your brain works in a way you hadn't had words for before? You don't have to name a diagnosis. Just notice what resonated.
  • What is one small habit or adjustment you'll try? Why is it realistic for your life right now, and how will you know if it's working?
A strong answer

Names one specific thing you're learning about yourself and why it matters, and describes one small, realistic habit with a clear way to tell whether it's working. Honest beats polished.

How it's scored

Full credit goes to responses that are honest, specific, and self-aware, written in your own voice using complete sentences and a college-appropriate tone. Generic answers that could apply to anyone won't earn full credit. The goal is self-knowledge, not a perfect plan.

Mile Marker scoring guide
ScoreWhat it means
4Strong ProgressYour response is thoughtful, specific, and clearly connected to the purpose of the Mile Marker. You show strong reflection, effort, and engagement with the topic.
3On TrackYour response addresses the main parts of the task and shows reflection and engagement. Some ideas could be explained in more detail or connected more clearly.
2DevelopingYour response is partially complete or too general. More detail, reflection, or clearer connections are needed.
1Needs RevisionYour response is very limited, incomplete, or unclear and does not fully address the task.
0No EvidenceNo submission was provided, or the response does not meaningfully engage with the task.
