Lesson 1.3

What's AI, Really?

AI is already part of your daily life. This lesson is about using it thoughtfully.

About 5 minutes 3 of 8 in this module

You already use AI. You may not call it that, but you do.

Every time you ask a voice assistant something, get a "you might also like" recommendation, or see a summary at the top of a search page, something behind the scenes is deciding what you see. That something is artificial intelligence.

AI isn't a robot, and it isn't magic. The chat tools most people mean when they say "AI" are systems trained on enormous amounts of human-written text. They've gotten very good at producing responses that sound confident, authoritative, and complete.

Key term

Artificial intelligence (AI): computer systems that do tasks we usually connect with human thinking, like answering questions, writing, or recognizing patterns.

Sounding confident isn't the same as being right.

AI doesn't know you. It doesn't know your situation, your goals, or whether the answer it just gave you actually applies to your life. It produces what's statistically likely to be useful, which is often helpful and occasionally very, very wrong.

The part that trips people up

AI isn't "wrong" the way a calculator is wrong. A calculator that says 2 + 2 = 5 is broken: it tried to compute the right answer and failed. AI doesn't really work that way.

Try it

Hum this bassline: bum, bum, bum, buttabum, bum… (Bear with me.)

Now ask a few different people what song it is. Some will say Queen and David Bowie's "Under Pressure." Others, depending on their age and what they grew up with, will say Vanilla Ice's "Ice Ice Baby." Neither person is wrong. They both recognized a real pattern and completed it in the direction that was most familiar to them.

That's surprisingly close to what an AI chat tool does. It isn't looking up a fact and checking whether it's true. It's recognizing patterns in language and producing the most likely next words, based on everything it was trained on. Most of the time, that's accurate and useful. But sometimes the answer that sounds most likely isn't the correct one. It's just the one that fits the pattern best.

When AI produces something that sounds completely believable but isn't true, it's called a hallucination. It's not a glitch that will eventually go away. It's what happens when a pattern-completing system runs into a gap and fills it with whatever fits.

So when something an AI tells you seems off, the question isn't "did it make a mistake?" It's this: that sounded right, but is sounding right the same as being right?

That's why how you use AI matters more than whether you use it.

Key term

Hallucination: when AI produces information that sounds believable but is false, such as a fact, quote, or source that doesn't exist.

Five ways to show up thoughtfully

This course uses five behaviors to describe what thoughtful AI use looks like. You'll see them throughout the book. Two of them, BE Critical and BE Reflective, come up again and again, because they're the habits that travel with you into every course, every job, and every situation where someone is trying to tell you something.

  • BE Safe

    Know what you're sharing and with whom. Your personal information, financial details, and private circumstances stay private.

  • BE Honest

    There's a real difference between getting help from a tool and letting a tool do your thinking for you. Be clear about which one you're doing.

  • BE Critical

    Don't take AI output at face value. Check it. Ask where it came from. Notice when something seems off. This is the most transferable habit you'll build.

  • BE Responsible

    Make intentional choices about when AI supports your learning and when it gets in the way. Knowing the difference is the skill.

  • BE Reflective

    Notice what happens to your thinking when you use AI. Are you learning more, or less? Are you more confident in your own ideas, or less?

These five behaviors describe who you want to be as an AI user. The next lesson turns them into something you can do, every time you use AI for coursework. In Blackboard, the BE Check asks you to match each behavior to a real situation.

Your choice to make

You don't have to love AI. You don't have to use it. What this course asks is simpler than that: think before you act, and pay attention to what you're learning and how.

That's true of AI. It's true of everything else in this book, too.
