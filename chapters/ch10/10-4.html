Lesson 10.4

Strengths & Struggles in Collaboration

Every style has strengths and challenges. Knowing yours is power.

About 6 minutes 5 of 9 in this module

Every collaboration style has strengths. Every style has challenges. Knowing yours is power.

Now that you've explored your 16Personalities results, it's time to connect them to how you work with others: what you bring to a team, what drains you, how you communicate, where misunderstandings happen, and what you need to do your best work. Collaboration isn't about good or bad personality types. The more you understand your tendencies, the easier it gets.

Your type and collaboration

Each tendency tends to bring a strength to a group, and a struggle to watch for. Find your letters.

Collaboration strengths and struggles by personality trait
TraitStrengthStruggle to watch for
Extraverted (E)Energizes the group, brings ideas forwardMay talk over quieter members
Introverted (I)Thoughtful insight, calm presenceMay feel overshadowed or unheard
Observant (S)Practical, detail-oriented, groundedMay get frustrated by vague or "too theoretical" plans
Intuitive (N)Creative solutions, big-picture thinkingMay skip steps or overlook details
Thinking (T)Logic, clarity, fairnessMay come across as blunt or detached
Feeling (F)Empathy, harmony, connectionMay avoid conflict, even when it's needed
Judging (J)Organization, structure, follow-throughMay get stressed when plans change
Prospecting (P)Flexible, adaptable, spontaneousMay put things off or resist schedules
None of these are weaknesses. They're tendencies. Every team needs all kinds.

Different brains, different teammates

Not everyone's brain works the same way, and that's normal. Some students identify as neurodivergent (ADHD, autism, dyslexia, anxiety-related processing differences, and more). Others simply notice they think or work differently from the people around them. You do not need a diagnosis to recognize a pattern.

Look back at

Lesson 3.6: Your Brain, Your Way covers how these patterns show up in time and energy, too.

Strengths different brains may bring

  • Deep focus on meaningful tasks
  • Creativity and big-picture thinking
  • Spotting patterns quickly
  • Thinking outside the box
  • Honesty, clarity, and directness
  • Deep empathy or attention to detail

Challenges that may come up

  • Overwhelm in noisy or chaotic groups
  • Difficulty switching tasks
  • Trouble with timing, planning, or transitions
  • Missing social cues, or tone in texts
  • Frustration with vague expectations
  • Anxiety that affects speaking up
  • Shutdown or overload moments in group settings

Knowing your patterns, whether they come from personality, experience, or how your brain is wired, helps you ask for what you need and choose strategies that work for you. Knowing that other people have patterns too helps you collaborate more respectfully and patiently.

What you share about yourself, and with whom, is always your choice. You can name what helps ("I work best with written deadlines") without explaining why.

Name your strengths

Here are common collaboration strengths. Which ones fit you? You're probably strong in more than one.

  • Clear communicator
  • Supportive teammate
  • Creative problem-solver
  • Organized planner
  • Calm mediator
  • Detail-checker
  • Energizer or motivator
  • Deep thinker
  • Flexible adapter
  • Relationship-builder
  • Big-picture visionary
  • Dependable finisher

Check off yours, and the struggles you recognize, in Step 3 of the Collaboration Snapshot. The next lesson gives you strategies for working with both.
