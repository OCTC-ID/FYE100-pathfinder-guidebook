Mile Marker #5: Understanding My Money

The assignment, with support, and a form you can fill in and save.

About 10 minutes 6 of 7 in this module

This isn't about having a perfect budget. It's about knowing yourself a little better when it comes to money.

Leave this module with one honest insight and one realistic intention. There are no right or wrong financial situations here, only honest and not-so-honest responses.

●○AI use: AI-supported

You may use AI as a thinking partner to explore your ideas. Work with what it gives you, then write your responses in your own voice. Never share real account numbers, income, or other personal financial details with an AI tool.

See the AI Course Policy in the syllabus. Want a place to start? Try the optional AI thinking partner prompt.

Question 1

What You Noticed

After completing the Dollar Tracker, what did you learn about your spending patterns? What surprised you, or confirmed something you already suspected?

  • What category took more than you expected?
  • Where did small purchases add up?
  • What does your spending tell you about your actual priorities right now, even if they're different from what you'd say out loud?
A strong answer

Names one real pattern from your tracker, like a category or a kind of small purchase, and says what it shows about your actual priorities.

Question 2

Your Financial Reality

Describe your current financial situation honestly, not for judgment but for clarity. You don't need to share specific numbers.

  • What does your financial life look like right now: tight, manageable, comfortable, complicated, somewhere in between?
  • What's the biggest financial pressure you're dealing with this term?
  • What's one financial resource, habit, or support you already have that's helping, even a little?
  • Which other part of your wellness (like emotional, physical, or social) does money affect most for you right now?
  • In your own words, what does financial wellness mean to you, and how does money stress, or the lack of it, affect your schoolwork?
A strong answer

Describes your situation honestly without numbers, names your biggest pressure and one support, defines financial wellness in your own words, and connects money to your schoolwork and one other part of your wellness.

Question 3

One Realistic Next Step

Based on what you noticed this week, what's one small, realistic thing you want to do differently, or keep doing, when it comes to money?

  • It doesn't have to be dramatic. It just has to be honest and doable for your actual life right now.
  • Why does this feel like the right next step for where you are?
A strong answer

Names one specific step, connects it to something you noticed, and explains why it's realistic for your life right now.

Question 4

Your Zero-Based Budget

Look at the zero-based budget you built in the Budget Builder. You don't need to share any dollar amounts.

  • Which categories did you give jobs to, and why those?
  • What did giving every dollar a job show you that you hadn't noticed before?
A strong answer

Explains why you chose your categories and names one thing that building the budget showed you. No dollar amounts needed.

Optional: AI thinking partner

If you'd like help thinking through your answers, try this prompt in Microsoft Copilot or another AI tool:

Try this prompt

"Ask me questions to help me understand my spending patterns and identify one realistic change I could make, without sharing any personal financial details."

Use what's useful. Your final responses must be in your own words.

Keep it private

Never share account numbers, income amounts, debt amounts, or other sensitive financial information with an AI tool. Use general descriptions only: "I spend a lot on food," not "I spend $347 a month on groceries."

How it's scored

Full credit goes to responses that are honest, specific, and self-aware, written in your own voice. Generic answers that could apply to anyone won't earn full credit. There are no right or wrong financial situations. The goal is honest self-knowledge, not a perfect money plan.

Mile Marker scoring guide
ScoreWhat it means
4Strong ProgressYour response is thoughtful, specific, and clearly connected to the purpose of the Mile Marker. You show strong reflection, effort, and engagement with the topic.
3On TrackYour response addresses the main parts of the task and shows reflection and engagement. Some ideas could be explained in more detail or connected more clearly.
2DevelopingYour response is partially complete or too general. More detail, reflection, or clearer connections are needed.
1Needs RevisionYour response is very limited, incomplete, or unclear and does not fully address the task.
0No EvidenceNo submission was provided, or the response does not meaningfully engage with the task.
