Question 1

Your Type, Strengths & Challenges

What's your 16Personalities type? Describe it in your own words: one short phrase, not the official description. Then name two collaboration strengths and one or two challenges that honestly fit you.

Example: "INFJ-T: quiet planner who likes meaningful work. I'm an organized planner and a calm problem-solver, but I tend to avoid conflict and take on too much."

Strengths word bank: clear communicator · organized planner · calm problem-solver · creative idea generator · supportive teammate · detail-checker · mediator · flexible and adaptable · big-picture thinker · dependable follow-through

Challenges word bank: hard to get started · taking on too much · hard to speak up · avoiding conflict · too blunt · overwhelmed by chaos · frustrated by unclear instructions · perfectionism · withdrawing under stress · trouble reading tone

A strong answer

Gives your type in your own words, and names two strengths and one or two challenges that sound like you, not like a quiz summary.

Question 2

Why, and What Helps

Pick one challenge from Question 1 and explain why it happens for you. Think of it as a tendency, not a flaw: needing structure, being introverted, processing fast or slow, group anxiety, or how your brain works. (Sharing anything about neurodivergence is always optional.)

  • Then name three strategies that help you work better with others.
  • For one of them, describe a real group experience where it helped, or would have helped.

Example: "Group chaos overwhelms me because I process information better when expectations are clear."

Strategies word bank: setting expectations early · asking clarifying questions · breaking tasks into smaller pieces · using a shared doc · choosing a role that fits my strengths · pausing before reacting · setting mini-deadlines · being honest about what I need

A strong answer

Explains the why behind one challenge, names three strategies that fit you, and connects one to a real experience.

Question 3

What You Need From Your Team

In one sentence, simple and respectful, what do you need from teammates to do your best work?

Examples: "I work best when tasks and deadlines are clearly divided." "I thrive when I can think before speaking."

  • Optional: What does it look like when that need is met, and when it isn't?
A strong answer

Is one clear sentence that's specific, respectful, and something you could actually say to a real team.
