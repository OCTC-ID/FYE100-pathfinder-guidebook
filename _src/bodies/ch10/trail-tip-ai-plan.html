Using AI to turn "someone should probably..." into an actual plan.

Here's a scene almost everyone in a group project has lived through. The group agrees the project needs a topic, research, slides, and a presentation. Everyone nods. Nobody assigns anything. A week goes by, and now it's due Friday and somehow it's everyone's problem at once.

A lot of group tension isn't about people being lazy or difficult. It's that nobody wants to be the one who assigns tasks to other people. It can feel bossy or awkward. So nothing gets assigned, and the group just hopes.

AI is great at being the "bad guy" who hands out the to-do list, so no one in your group has to be.

This Trail Tip shows you how to use AI to break an assignment into tasks, suggest roles, and build a realistic timeline, fast. Your group still makes the real decisions. AI just gets you past the awkward "okay... so... who's doing what?" silence.

How to do it

  1. Check the AI rules first

    Look at the assignment's AI use label and the course AI policy. Planning with AI is usually fine even when the work itself has to be your own, but check.

  2. Paste in the assignment instructions

    The more AI knows about what's actually required, the more useful the plan will be.

  3. Add your group's roles and the due date

    Use "Member A, Member B" instead of names, or ask your group before you share their first names. Their information isn't yours to share.

  4. Ask for tasks, roles, a timeline, and group norms

    Use the prompt below. All four together turn a vague assignment into something your group can act on today.

  5. Bring the plan back to your group, and adjust it

    AI doesn't know who's great at design or who works on Tuesdays. Use the plan as a first draft, not a final ruling.

Try this prompt

"We're working on a group project for [class]. Here are the assignment instructions: [paste]. There are [number] of us, Member A through Member [letter], and the project is due [date]. Please build a simple group work plan with: 1) the main tasks, 2) suggested roles so everyone contributes, 3) a weekly timeline, 4) a few earlier check-in dates so we're not rushing at the end, and 5) four or five simple group norms for how we'll communicate and stay organized. Keep it simple, realistic, and student-friendly, with headings and bullet points."

AI Literacy BE Responsible BE Safe

The plan is the easy part

AI can generate a plan in seconds. Following through on it is still on your group. A plan nobody looks at again isn't more useful than no plan at all.

This is BE Responsible in action: using AI to handle an awkward, low-value task so your group has more time and energy for the parts that need your thinking. And it's BE Safe, too: keep your teammates' names and details out of the prompt unless they've said it's okay.

If your group divides the work with AI's help, the work itself still follows the assignment's AI use label. Planning with AI doesn't make AI a group member.
