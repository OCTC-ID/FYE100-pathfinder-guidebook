Picking a program isn't just picking a subject you like. It's choosing a credential, a timeline, and a direction.

Those choices shape what you take, when you can finish, and where you can go next. Most students never get a clear explanation of how this works before they register for their first class. This lesson fixes that: how credentials stack at OCTC, how your Career Coach results connect to programs, and what to bring to your Success Coach.

How credentials stack

At OCTC, many credentials are designed to build on each other. Courses are packaged into certificates. Certificates can stack into diplomas. Diplomas can stack into degrees. And transfer degrees lead to four-year universities. You don't always have to start at the bottom, and you don't have to stop at the top.

[[term]] Key Term

Stackable credential: a credential whose courses also count toward the next, bigger credential.

What each credential means

Credit hours and length vary by program, so check the specific program in the KCTCS catalog.

Certificate

  • The fastest path to a specific skill or entry-level credential
  • Often stackable: the credits count toward a diploma or AAS
  • Good for getting into the workforce quickly or adding a skill
  • Some can be finished in a semester or two

Diploma

  • More in-depth preparation than a certificate
  • Common in technical and health programs
  • Credits often apply toward an AAS if you keep going
  • Usually about one to two years of full-time study

AAS degree

  • The full career-ready credential for technical and professional programs
  • Usually about two years of full-time study
  • Includes general education courses alongside your major courses
  • Some programs have transfer agreements. Ask your Success Coach.

AA or AS degree

  • Designed to transfer to Kentucky's four-year universities
  • Associate in Arts (AA) for humanities, social sciences, and similar fields
  • Associate in Science (AS) for science and math fields
  • Built to count toward a bachelor's degree, protecting the work you've already done

An AAS isn't a dead end. It's designed for the workforce, but many AAS graduates go on to a four-year university by taking additional general education courses, and some universities have agreements with KCTCS colleges that make the path clearer. If you might want a bachelor's degree someday, ask your Success Coach about transfer options before you assume it isn't possible.

[[tip]] Pathfinder Tip

In a technical program, ask your advisor: "Does this certificate stack toward a diploma or AAS?" You may be closer to the next credential than you think, and able to earn something with job value before you finish.

What to bring to your Success Coach

Your Success Coach is your academic GPS. Registration for next term comes up fast, and the most valuable thing you can do before that appointment is arrive with something specific to talk about.

  • [[tick]]Your Career Coach results: your interest types and top matches. Your coach can help you read them in light of OCTC's actual programs.
  • [[tick]]One or two programs you want to know more about. Not a final decision, just a starting point.
  • [[tick]]Specific questions. "What should I take?" versus "I'm interested in nursing. What do I need to finish this term to stay on track for the next application deadline?" is the difference between a routine appointment and a useful one.

Mile Marker #6 asks you to write those questions. Don't wait until you're sitting across from your advisor to figure out what you need to know.
