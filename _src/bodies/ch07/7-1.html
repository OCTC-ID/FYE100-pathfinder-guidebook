Adaptability & Leadership

What this skill is

This isn't about a title. It's about how you show up, especially when things change.

Adaptability & Leadership sounds like it belongs to people with corner offices and formal authority. It doesn't. At the level this course focuses on, it's about something much more immediate: contributing to a shared goal, adjusting when circumstances shift, and helping others stay on track when things get hard.

You probably already do versions of all three. This module helps you see where and how.

Leadership is a pattern of behavior more than a position, and you've been practicing it longer than you think.

Where you're headed

Here's the difference between where most students start and where you're headed, in plain terms.

[[see]] See also

Lesson 1.2 explains Benchmark, Milestone, and Capstone.

  1. Benchmark Where you're starting

    • You can describe how people cooperate to reach a common goal.
    • You can explain how new situations may require doing things differently.
    • You recognize how people motivate others to reach a goal.

  2. Milestone Your target

    • You contribute toward reaching a common goal.
    • You adjust to new situations by doing things differently and keeping a positive mindset.
    • You encourage others to keep going toward their goals as situations change.

The shift from Benchmark to Milestone is the shift from understanding how these things work to actually doing them, in real situations, with real people, under real pressure.

What leadership actually looks like

Most students hear "leadership" and think of student government, team captains, or supervisors. But the Milestone level doesn't require any of that. It requires showing up: contributing, adjusting, and encouraging, in whatever setting you're already in.

[[x]]

What people assume it means

  • Being in charge of a group
  • Having a title or formal role
  • Being the loudest or most confident person
  • Making all the decisions
  • Never struggling or needing help
[[check]]

What it actually looks like

  • Showing up consistently and following through
  • Adjusting your approach when something isn't working
  • Helping a teammate who's stuck, without being asked
  • Staying steady when a plan falls apart
  • Contributing your strengths to something shared

Where it shows up in this module

This module's self-assessment work connects directly to Adaptability & Leadership. That's why it's the featured skill here.

Strengths: how you adapt

Your strengths say a lot about how you naturally respond to change and challenge. A student who leads through relationships adapts differently than one who leads through analysis. Neither is wrong, but knowing which one you are helps you use it on purpose instead of stumbling into it.

Values: why you lead

Your values are the reason you show up for things, the "why" behind your choices and commitments. When circumstances change and motivation gets harder, values are what anchor you. They're also what makes your leadership real rather than a performance.

Blind spots: where to watch

Every strength has an overuse pattern, and an overused strength can get in the way of adapting. The student who leads through decisiveness may struggle to adjust when the first plan fails. Naming your blind spot is the first step to managing it when things get hard.

Self-knowledge: better leadership

It's hard to lead others well if you don't understand yourself. The HIGH5 assessment and the Values Card Sort aren't just personal reflection. They're leadership groundwork, and what you learn about yourself applies directly to how you show up for others.

In your own life

[[pen]] Reflect
  • Think of a time, this semester or before college, when something changed unexpectedly and you had to find a different approach. What did you do? What does that tell you about how you adapt?
  • Is there someone in your life, at work, at home, or in a group, whom you quietly help stay on track or keep going? That's leadership. What strengths make you that person for them?
  • When things get hard, what do you fall back on? A value, a habit, a person? Watch for where that anchor shows up in your results in this module.

How it shows up later

Adaptability & Leadership is one of the three assigned skills for your STAR(T) Stories at the end of the course. You'll write or record one story about a specific time you adapted, contributed, or helped others keep going through change.

The self-knowledge you build in this module is the foundation of that story. It doesn't have to be dramatic. A moment when you kept going when you wanted to quit, and your persistence helped someone else keep going too, is a Milestone-level story. Start small. Start honest. Start noticing those moments now. The STAR(T) Stories Guide at the back of this book walks you through the whole capstone, with a practice guide for each story.
