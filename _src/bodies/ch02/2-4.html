Your KCTCS email is how OCTC contacts you, not your personal Gmail or Yahoo account.

Missing messages can mean missing deadlines, financial aid updates, or important class announcements. Knowing how to get to your email, check it regularly, and write professionally keeps you on the right path from day one.

Get to your KCTCS email

On a computer

  1. Log in to MyPath with your KCTCS username and password.
  2. Select the Email tile (it shows the Outlook logo). This is the easiest way in.
  3. Your inbox opens. It's automatically linked to your college account.

Another way in: once you're signed in to any Microsoft page, select the app launcher (the grid of nine dots, sometimes called the "waffle") in the upper-left corner. It opens all your Microsoft apps in the browser, including Outlook, Word, OneDrive, and Teams.

On your phone

Install the Outlook app and sign in with your KCTCS account. Turn on notifications so you don't miss a message.

Communicate professionally

College email isn't like texting. It reflects your professional self, and instructors notice the difference.

1

Address people correctly

Use a respectful title and last name: Professor, Dr., Mr., or Ms. Don't use first names unless your instructor has said it's okay. When in doubt, "Professor" works for any college instructor.

2

Write a subject line

The subject line is the short title you type in the Subject box, above your message. It's the first thing your instructor sees in their inbox, before they open your email.

An email isn't a text. Your message doesn't go in the subject line. The subject line is a few words that say what the email is about, and your actual question goes in the big box below it.

A good subject line is short, specific, and includes your course. Your instructor can tell what you need before opening the email, and can find it again later.

[[term]] Key term

Subject line: the short title of an email. It tells the reader what the email is about. The message itself goes in the body below.

3

Write a clear, complete message

Every professional email has the same basic parts. Skip text shortcuts, emojis, and slang, even if your instructor seems casual in class.

Greeting
"Hello Dr. Smith,"
Introduction
"My name is Jordan Lee, and I'm in your online FYE 100, Section 201."
Purpose
"I wanted to ask whether the reflection assignment has a rubric."
Closing
"Thank you," followed by your full name
4

Stay respectful and calm

If you're upset about a grade, a deadline, or a situation, step away before you reply. Reread your email out loud and ask yourself: would I say this in person?

Once you hit Send, you can't take the words back. Your instructor is a real person who wants to help you succeed.

5

Be specific and prepared

  • Include the course name and section number in every email. Instructors often teach several sections.
  • Check the syllabus and announcements first. Your question may already be answered there, and showing that you looked goes a long way.

What makes the good email work

  1. A clear subject line says what the email is about before it's even opened.
  2. Greeting and title show respect.
  3. A friendly opener adds a human connection.
  4. Course and section tell your instructor who you are.
  5. A clear, complete question is easy to answer.
  6. Evidence of effort shows you checked the syllabus and asked a classmate.
  7. A specific request says exactly what you need.
  8. A polite closing gives a professional finish.

Professional communication builds respect with instructors and staff, gets you answers faster, and practices skills you'll use in every job you ever have.

Every email you send is a small but real picture of who you are as a student and a professional. Make it count.
