You can type your stories. But if you can bring yourself to record them, even audio only, even nervous, you'll get something typing can't give you.

In a real interview, you'll be talking, not typing. Recording your STAR(T) Stories is the low-stakes version of that moment. Saying your story out loud, in a clear order, is the practice.

This isn't a public speaking class

Recordings are not graded on delivery or presentation. No one is scoring your eye contact, your voice, your lighting, or your camera. They're scoring your story and its structure. It only needs to be clear enough to follow.

If you stumble, keep going. Interviewers don't expect polish either. They listen for honesty, structure, and a clear point.

The rambling problem

Even experienced professionals ramble when they're nervous. They back up to add context, circle around the point, and suddenly realize they've talked for four minutes without reaching the Action. That's nerves, not a lack of preparation.

A tight two-minute story that hits all five steps beats a wandering seven-minute one every time. The fix is simple: know your story before you hit record. Read your practice guide out loud at least once. Know where each step starts and ends. Then trust the structure.

[[tip]] Pathfinder Tip

Practice once with a timer. Running long? Your Action or your Situation is doing too much. Trim the Situation first. Each practice guide also estimates how long your draft runs out loud.

Your options

You choose, story by story.

Video, camera on

Closest to a real interview. Face a window for good light, and look at the camera, not the screen.

Audio only

Your voice, no video. Completely fine. A headphone mic often sounds better than your device's built-in one.

Record on your phone

Record, then upload. A good backup if the Blackboard recorder gives you trouble. Prop the phone steady, sideways for video.

Typed response

Type directly into the submission box. Document uploads aren't accepted. All five steps are still required. The practice guides can copy your draft for you.

Recording in Blackboard

Blackboard has a built-in recorder, Video Studio. You don't need a separate account or download.

  1. Open the submission box

    In the STAR(T) Stories portal for the skill you're submitting, find the camera button in the editor toolbar.

  2. Choose camera or audio

    Allow your browser to use your camera or microphone when it asks.

  3. Record

    Select Start Recording. There's a short countdown. You can pause, resume, or retake as you go.

  4. Preview, then save

    Watch or listen once. If you want another try, retake it. When you're happy, save it and submit.

You have plenty of room: Video Studio recordings can run up to 30 minutes. Your target is still 2 to 4.

[[tip]] Find a quiet spot
