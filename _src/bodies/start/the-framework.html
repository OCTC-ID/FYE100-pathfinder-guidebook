Anyone can claim a skill. A STAR(T) story shows it.

STAR comes from behavioral interviewing, the method many employers use when they want to know not just what you know, but what you've actually done. When an interviewer says "Tell me about a time when...", they're asking for a STAR story. "I'm a hard worker" is a claim. A specific story is evidence.

This course adds one more step, Transfer, the (T). It turns a good story into a reflection on where you're headed.

The five steps

  1. S

    Situation: set the scene

    Briefly describe where you were and what was happening. Give just enough background for someone who wasn't there.

    What was going on?

    Watch out: don't linger. The Situation is setup, not the story.

  2. T

    Task: define your role

    What were you responsible for? What were you trying to accomplish, and why was it yours to handle?

    What did you need to do, and why was it on you?

    Watch out: your task is your part, not the group's general goal.

  3. A

    Action: show what you did

    The most important step. Use "I": I decided, I reached out, I applied, I chose. Describe what you did, not what the team did or what happened in general. The Action is where the skill lives.

    What did you specifically do, step by step?

    Watch out: "we" hides your contribution. Describe your part, even in a group effort.

  4. R

    Result: name the outcome

    What happened because of what you did? Results don't have to be perfect. An honest, imperfect result is more believable than a vague positive one.

    What happened, and what did it mean?

    Watch out: "It went well" isn't a result. Name what changed.

  5. T

    Transfer: connect it forward

    So what? What does this experience mean for your next term, your career, or your life beyond college? This is what makes a STAR(T) story a reflection, not just a story.

    What does this mean for what comes next?

    Watch out: "This will help me in the future" isn't a Transfer. Say where, when, and how.

[[term]] Key Term

Behavioral interview: a job interview built on "Tell me about a time when..." questions, where your past actions are the evidence.

[[tip]] Why the T?

Standard STAR ends with the Result, which is enough for an interview. Transfer shows you learned from the experience and know where you're taking it. It's the hardest step for most students, and the one that most sets a strong story apart.
