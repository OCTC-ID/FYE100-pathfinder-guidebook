Most stressful decisions don't come from the decision itself. They come from confusion.

"What should I major in?" "Should I drop this class?" "Should I take that job?" "Should I bring this up or let it go?" Critical thinking gets easier when you have a simple tool to walk through a decision step by step. That's the 4C Check.

The four Cs

  1. C1

    Clarity: What am I really deciding?

    A lot of poor decisions come from being unclear about the actual question. Strong feelings and quick assumptions can blur it, so name them and set them aside for a moment.

    • "Do I hate this class, or am I just overwhelmed this week?"
    • "Am I unsure about the major, or just nervous about one course?"
    • "Is the relationship the problem, or the way we communicate?"

    Ask: What is the exact decision I'm trying to make?

  2. C2

    Context: What matters most here?

    Name your priorities, values, and limits, and consider how the situation looks from someone else's point of view.

    • "My priority is finishing on time."
    • "I need flexibility because of childcare."
    • "I value honesty, even when it's awkward."
    • "How would my instructor see this?"

    Ask: What factors matter most for me right now, and whose view am I missing?

  3. C3

    Credibility: What do I know, and what am I assuming?

    Check the quality of your information. Separate what you know from what you're assuming or have only heard.

    • Solid: your advisor, the catalog, the syllabus, official information. "MAT 150 is required for my program."
    • Shaky: rumors, guesses, assumptions. "Someone said this instructor is impossible."

    "AI told me this is the transfer requirement. Did I check it against the actual catalog?"

    Ask: What do I know for sure, and what do I need to check?

  4. C4

    Consequences: What might happen next?

    Name your realistic options and think one step ahead for each, not ten. Then choose the most reasonable next step.

    • "If I drop this class, will it delay when I finish?"
    • "If I send this message, will it help or make things worse?"
    • "If I take this job, how will it affect my schedule?"

    "If I let AI write my whole email to my instructor, how will I sound, and how will I answer if they write back with a question?"

    Ask: What are my options, what's likely to happen with each, and what's my next step?

[[tip]] Pathfinder Tip

You won't always need all four. Sometimes naming the real question (Clarity) or checking your information (Credibility) is enough to see things differently. Save the full check for decisions that feel bigger or stickier than they should.

Practice it: Think It Through

In your course, you'll practice the 4C Check with Coach Pathfinder, an AI coach who walks you through each C one at a time. Coach Pathfinder asks questions. It doesn't hand you answers. Your job is to actually think it through, not guess what the AI wants to hear.

You'll choose a scenario, or bring a real situation of your own:

  • The confusing assignment: unclear instructions, with the deadline getting close.
  • The group project problem: a teammate isn't responding, and the deadline is coming up.
  • The technology fail: you submitted in Blackboard, but you aren't sure it uploaded.

Plan for about four to six exchanges and a short reflection at the end. Be honest. There's no "right" answer here, just real thinking.

The 4C Check is this course's tool. It's built on Richard Paul and Linda Elder's Elements of Reasoning and Intellectual Standards (Foundation for Critical Thinking): Clarity draws on the question at issue and the standard of clarity, Context on purpose and point of view, Credibility on information, assumptions, and accuracy, and Consequences on implications and consequences.
