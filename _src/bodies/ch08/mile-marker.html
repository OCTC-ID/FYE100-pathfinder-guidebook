One career. Honest research. Your real assessment of the fit.

You've done the research. This Mile Marker asks you to make sense of it: not repeat it, but honestly assess what you found and what it means for you.

It's also how the college assesses Professionalism (#6), one of the course's focus skills. That doesn't mean it needs to sound polished. It needs to sound like you, and be specific and honest.

●○AI use: AI-supported

You may use AI as a thinking partner to question your assumptions about a career. Get your facts from your research sources, not from AI, and write your responses in your own voice.

See the AI Course Policy in the syllabus. Want a place to start? Try the optional AI thinking partner prompt.

Part 2: Respond to each question

Question 1

What Did You Find?

Summarize the career in your own words: education required, regional salary, job outlook, and typical work environment. Keep it brief. The guide has the detail. This is the headline version.

A strong answer

Covers all four: education, regional salary, outlook, and work environment, in your own words.

Question 2

Does It Fit?

Connect your research to what you know about yourself from Modules 6 and 7.

  • Where do your strengths show up in this career?
  • Where are your values honored, and where is there friction?
  • How does the work setting fit how you work best?
  • How does the career's pace and pressure compare with how much pressure you handle well?
  • How does the day-to-day reality fit your life?
A strong answer

Connects specific research findings to your strengths, your values, how you work best, and your pressure tolerance. Not just a list of your results.

Question 3

What's Your Honest Take?

Name one clear area of alignment and one area of friction or uncertainty.

  • What's the one question you still need answered before you could commit more seriously to this direction?
  • Who or what could help you answer it?
A strong answer

Names one alignment and one friction, asks one specific question, and names a person or resource that could help answer it.

Optional: AI thinking partner

If you'd like help testing your thinking, try this prompt in Microsoft Copilot or another AI tool:

Try this prompt

"I'm a college student exploring a career as a [career]. My top strengths include [two or three strengths], and I value [two or three values]. Ask me three hard questions that would help me test whether this career really fits me. Don't answer them for me."

Use the questions to push your thinking, then write your responses in your own words.

[[alert]] Check it against your research

Don't use AI for salary, outlook, or education requirements. Those come from Career Coach, O*NET, and the Occupational Outlook Handbook. Leave your name and other personal details out of the prompt.
