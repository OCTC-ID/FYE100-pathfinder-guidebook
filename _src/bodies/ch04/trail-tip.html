OneNote: your digital Trapper Keeper Start here

OneNote is a free-form digital notebook organized into notebooks, sections, and pages. Think of it as a Trapper Keeper that lives in the cloud: you can open it on any device, it never gets left at home, and you can't lose pages because you forgot to put them back.

Unlike a Word document, OneNote isn't linear. You can type anywhere on the page, add images, paste links, record audio, and draw diagrams, and organize it all the way your brain works, not the way a template tells you to.

How to use it for school

  • One notebook per semester. Make a section for each class, then a page for each lecture or topic. Everything in one place.
  • Capture anything. Paste a screenshot of a slide, type your notes next to it, and add a question you want to follow up on. No formatting required.
  • Search everything. OneNote searches all of your notes. If you wrote it down, you can find it.
  • Share with study partners. Share a section with a classmate to take notes together without sending files back and forth.
  • Come back and add to it. Revisit old notes, add new thoughts, and mark what mattered. Your notes become a living document.
[[tip]] Pathfinder Tip

Set up a "Class Notes" notebook before your first class of the semester, with one section per course. Five minutes of setup pays off every week.

OneDrive: your files, everywhere

OneDrive is Microsoft's cloud storage, included with your KCTCS account. Every file you save there is backed up and available from any device where you sign in with your KCTCS account.

The most common academic disaster is a lost, broken, or forgotten file. OneDrive prevents most of those before they happen.

How to use it for school

  • Save everything there by default. Not just the important files. If you worked on it, it lives in OneDrive.
  • Organize by semester and course. A simple folder structure (Semester → Course → Assignments) takes two minutes to set up and saves hours of searching later.
  • Name files so you can find them. Pick one pattern and stick to it, like FYE100_MileMarker4_Draft. Future you will know exactly what's inside.
  • Share a link instead of an attachment. When sharing is allowed, a OneDrive link is cleaner than an email attachment, and you can update the file without resending it.
  • Get to it from anywhere. Forgot your laptop? Sign in on a library computer and everything is there.
[[tip]] Pathfinder Tip

On a device that isn't yours, like a library computer or a family member's laptop, save to OneDrive as you go, not just at the end. Sessions time out.

Teams: communication that isn't email

Teams is Microsoft's chat, video call, and file-sharing tool, all in one place. Some OCTC courses and programs use Teams for class communication, office hours, or group projects.

  • Check whether your instructor uses it. Some instructors hold virtual office hours or post updates in Teams.
  • Group projects. Instead of endless email threads, set up a Teams chat for your group. Share files, chat, and meet in one place.
  • Study sessions. Video calls with study partners are included with your KCTCS account.

Microsoft Copilot: AI with your school account

Microsoft Copilot is an AI assistant you can use with your KCTCS Microsoft account. It's a good place to start with AI as a student: when you're signed in with your KCTCS email, you're using it under your school account's privacy protections instead of a separate personal account.

Go to copilot.microsoft.com and sign in with your KCTCS email and password.

AI Literacy BE Responsible

Good ways to use Copilot for school

  • Brainstorming. "Give me five angles I could take on this essay topic." Then choose one and develop it yourself.
  • Explaining concepts. "Explain [topic] in plain language, then give me three questions I should be able to answer about it."
  • Reviewing your own notes. Paste in notes you wrote and ask for the key points. Then use that list to quiz yourself.
  • Feedback on clarity. "Read this paragraph and tell me if the main idea is clear." Then revise it yourself, in your own words.
  • Email drafting. "Help me write a professional email to my instructor asking about [topic]." Review it, revise it, and send it as your own.

Copilot, like any AI tool, sounds confident whether or not it's right. Check facts against your course materials or credible sources before you rely on them. Lesson 4.3 gives you a method for doing exactly that. And check the AI rules for each assignment first (Lesson 1.4).
