The Pathfinder Guidebook holds the readings for FYE 100. Blackboard holds everything that gets submitted, scored, or dated.

This part walks through how the book is put together, the design choices behind it, and a few ways to use it in online and in-person sections. The next three parts are alignment maps you can use for planning, peer review, and reporting.

The book and Blackboard

The book and your Blackboard course each do a different job. Keeping them separate lets the book stay the same from term to term while your course shell carries the details that change.

What lives in the book and what lives in Blackboard
In the bookIn Blackboard
Lessons, Skill Spotlights, and Trail TipsDue dates, points, and submission links
Trail Tools (fillable worksheets)Rubrics and grading
Mile Marker instructions and a fill-in formAI Chats with Coach Pathfinder
The Merit Guide and the STAR(T) Stories GuideQuizzes, discussions, and journals
Self-check practice with answers students can openExtra credit, announcements, and anything section-specific

Because the book is public, it never includes answer keys for graded work, scoring guidance, points, or dates. When students ask when something is due, the book sends them to Blackboard every time. That also means you can move a due date in your section without anything in the book going out of date.

[[tip]] Good to know

This guide follows the same rule. The maps show where each outcome is practiced and assessed, not how it's scored.

How a module is built

Every module follows the same pattern, so students learn the layout once and then spend their attention on the content.

  • Module overview

    Why the module matters, the "I Can" objectives (verbatim from the Competency Framework) with a tag showing where each one is shown, the 10 Essential Skills and AI Literacy behaviors in play, and a Merit reminder in Modules 4, 7, and 10.

  • Skill Spotlight

    Most modules open with a short lesson on one of the 10 Essential Skills, so every skill gets a dedicated introduction across the course.

  • Lessons

    Short readings with key terms, examples, self-check practice, and AI Literacy boxes tagged with the BE behavior they build.

  • Trail Tips

    Practical how-tos, like setting up Office 365, Merit, or Handshake, or figuring out how a grade is calculated.

  • Trail Tools

    Fillable worksheets that sit at the top of the module. Students fill them in as they read, and the work feeds the Mile Marker.

  • Mile Marker

    The module's main assignment: the prompt, support for each question, and a fill-in form. Each one carries an AI use label and saves to a PDF named "Mile Marker #N - Student Name".

  • Wrap Up

    Key takeaways, one reflection prompt, and a link to the next module.

Design commitments

  • Timeless. No dates, deadlines, or points, so the book works in every term and every section.
  • Students don't lose their place. Every link that leaves the book opens in a new tab. Links inside the book stay in the same tab.
  • Accessible by default. Real headings, alt text, keyboard-friendly forms, readable contrast, and print styles on every page.
  • Private by design. What students type into a Trail Tool or Mile Marker form stays in their own browser on that device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Drafts can disappear if a student clears browser data or switches devices, so the book reminds them to save a PDF.
  • Clear about AI. Every Mile Marker carries an AI use label, using the same three levels students learn in Lesson 1.4. AI is never required.
  • Written for students. Plain, warm, second-person language, with research claims checked against their sources.

Teaching with the book

Online sections

  • Link each Blackboard module to that module's overview page. Students can move through the whole module from there.
  • Students can submit a Mile Marker as the saved PDF, or copy their answers into the Blackboard submission. Tell them which you prefer.
  • Point students to the On-Demand Options in the Merit Guide if they can't get to campus.

In-person sections

  • Trail Tools work well as in-class activities. Students can fill them in on a laptop or phone, or you can print them.
  • The self-check practice in the lessons (for example, "spot the trap" in Lesson 9.3) makes a ready-made warm-up or discussion starter.
  • Printable Mile Marker handouts are available for students who'd rather work on paper.

Across the course

  • The Mile Markers build toward the STAR(T) Stories capstone. Students go back to them in Module 11 to choose their four stories. The STAR(T) Stories Guide has the framework and a practice guide for each story.
  • No assignment requires AI use. The AI Literacy behaviors are framed as judgment, including the decision not to use AI, which respects students with principled objections.

Keeping the book current

Most of the book is built to stay put. A few pieces change on purpose:

  • Merit On-Demand Options are refreshed each term as Career Services posts new workshops.
  • The alignment maps in this guide are generated from the same source as the book, so they update each time a module is added or revised. Modules still in development show as planned, using the Competency Framework.
  • Outside links are checked when modules are revised. If you find a broken link or an error, contact Stephanie Self, Instructional Designer & eLearning Coordinator.

Course-level assessment

Three pieces of the course produce evidence beyond a single module:

  • My Skills Snapshot (pre and post). A short self-assessment in Blackboard on the 10 Essential Skills and AI use, taken in Module 1 and again in Module 12. Students compare the two. Lesson 1.2 introduces it, and the Module 12 wrap-up sends students back to it.
  • Mile Marker #8: My Career Snapshot. The institutional anchor for SLO 3, through #6 Professionalism (I Can 8.2).
  • My STAR(T) Stories (capstone). The institutional anchor for SLO 1, through #9 Knowledge Application (I Can 12.1 and 12.2). Students name their four skills and experiences in Mile Marker #11 and submit the stories in the Capstone module in Module 12.

Mile Marker #1 and Mile Marker #12 also bookend the course: #12 asks students to compare directly to their Starting Point, including how they think about AI.
